## In my own words
A binary place value ladder always bottoms out at one, never at two. The rightmost bit is the ones place, which is literally the bit that says whether the number is odd. My first mistake was starting the ladder at two, which doubled every answer. Also, n bits all set to one equal two to the power n, minus one. The reason is that adding one to a row of all ones rolls over to the next power of two, so all ones is one less than that power. This is why an eight bit number tops out at 255, a sixteen bit number at 65535, and a thirty two bit number at about four point three billion. To go from decimal to binary, subtract the largest power of two that fits, mark a one, and repeat.
