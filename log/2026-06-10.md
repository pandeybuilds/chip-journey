## Hexadecimal
- Hexadecimal is binary in a readable form. One hex digit maps to exactly four bits because sixteen equals two to the fourth power. Converting is pure substitution by groups of four bits (nibbles), with no arithmetic. This is why memory addresses and registers are always shown in hex.
- Worked both directions. Binary to hex: 11110000 became F0, 10110101 became B5, 1100 became C. Hex to binary: 2F became 00101111, A7 became 10100111.

## Two's complement
- Two's complement stores negative numbers so that ordinary binary addition just works. To negate a value you flip every bit and add one. The reason it works is that flipping all bits of x gives (two to the n, minus one) minus x, so flipping and adding one gives two to the n, minus x, which is exactly the value that rolls over to zero when added to x. The most significant bit carries negative weight (minus eight in four bits), and the range is minus eight through plus seven, with only a single zero.
- The payoff: a processor has no subtractor. It computes a minus b as a plus the negation of b, reusing the same adder, and the carry simply falls off the end. One adder handles addition, subtraction, and the basis of multiplication and division.
- Worked examples in four bits. Negation: minus three is 1101, minus six is 1010. Reading signed values: 1110 is minus two, 1001 is minus seven. Subtraction as addition: five minus three becomes 0101 plus 1101, which gives 10010, and dropping the carry leaves 0010, which is plus two.
- Habit I picked up: after negating, read the result back through the place values to check it.

## Boolean algebra
- The three fundamentals are NOT, AND, and OR. AND means both, OR means either, XOR means the two inputs differ. NAND and NOR are AND and OR with the output flipped. Truth tables are finite, so every rule can be verified directly rather than trusted.
- De Morgan's laws, in the version I want to keep for life: to beat an AND, beat either input; to beat an OR, beat both inputs. Mechanically: break the bar, flip the operator between AND and OR, and negate each piece separately. The piece I have to consciously check is negating every term, not just the first one. My memory anchors are the bouncer (needs the list and an identity card, so you are rejected if you are not on the list or you have no identity card) and the laptop (buy it if it is cheap and fast, so you will not buy it if it is not cheap or it is not fast).
- NAND universality: every logic function can be built from NAND gates alone. NOT is NAND of a signal with itself. AND is a NAND followed by a NAND wired as a NOT. OR is NAND of (NAND of A with itself) and (NAND of B with itself), which is really De Morgan's law in disguise. This is the foundation that Nand2Tetris in L2 is built on.
